# v0.4.4 — Syncthing upload status that reflects reality

When you quit a game, the plugin backs up your save and then tells you whether Syncthing has copied it to your other devices. That last part was never really working. **SYNCTHING COMPLETE** appeared on a timer about twenty seconds after the backup, whether or not any of your devices had actually received anything.

This release makes that status mean what it says.

## What changed for you

**COMPLETE now means a device really has your save.** The plugin watches your connected devices report their progress and keeps showing **SYNCTHING UPLOADING** until one of them confirms it has the backup. Before, it just waited a fixed length of time and then declared success.

**It waits for one device, not all of them.** Your fastest device usually has the save within a few seconds while a slower one lags well behind. Waiting for everything meant staring at the status strip for however long the slowest device took. Now it finishes once a connected device confirms it has the save and has held steady for a few checks.

This does mean COMPLETE is a slightly weaker promise than it sounds. Other devices may still be catching up when it appears, and devices that are switched off or offline are not covered at all. The status list in the README says this too.

**Tidying up old backups no longer holds things up.** Syncthing separates "still needs files" from "still deleting files it no longer needs". Only the first matters for whether your save arrived — the second is housekeeping as old Ludusavi snapshots get cleaned up. One run had every device holding the save after 24 seconds, then spent four and a half minutes on cleanup and finished by wrongly reporting the upload as incomplete. Cleanup is now ignored when deciding whether you are done.

**It stops waiting around once your folder is quiet.** Settling used to require fifteen seconds of no activity in the backup folder. Measurements showed the folder actually goes quiet about a tenth of a second after a backup, so most of that was dead time. End to end, a post-game sync went from 27 seconds to 18 on the same game.

**A genuinely slow upload says so.** If monitoring ends while a device is still catching up, you get **LOCAL BACKUP SAVED — SYNCTHING UPLOAD INCOMPLETE** rather than an error suggesting Syncthing is broken. Your backup is safe either way; Syncthing carries on in the background.

## Why it was broken

The plugin subscribes to Syncthing's event feed to see what your devices are doing. It was asking for those events using a position marker taken from a different feed, and the two number their events separately — so the marker pointed far past anything the plugin would ever be sent.

**The plugin was receiving no Syncthing events at all.** Everything it appeared to notice came from repeatedly asking for folder status instead. That is why upload activity was never detected and why COMPLETE could only ever be a timer. Fixing the marker is what made the rest of this release possible, and it was confirmed on a Steam Deck by running the old and new versions side by side against the same folder.

A related problem: Syncthing throws away idle event subscriptions, and a fresh one starts counting from the beginning. The plugin now notices when that happens instead of going quiet for the rest of the session.

## Diagnostics

Post-game upload activity is now logged when something changes, rather than on a timer: how many devices are connected, how many still need the save, how many are still cleaning up, and how much is outstanding. Device identifiers and folder paths are never written to the log. Turning on **Debug Logging** keeps the watcher reporting after COMPLETE so you can see propagation finish; it does not change what the status strip shows.

## Security

Three advisories in build-time dependencies (`postcss` and `nanoid`) were cleared by upgrading rather than by silencing them. One fix was newer than this project's two-week quarantine on dependency updates, so it was allowed through as a single pinned version — every other release of those packages stays quarantined and no advisory is ignored. These were never part of the plugin you install.

## Worth knowing

The upload path was tested on a Steam Deck with three connected devices across several sessions. The safety limits that stop a watch when an upload stalls or runs too long were never reached during that testing, so they remain unproven in practice.
